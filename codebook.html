Codebook

Six operational categories for analysing English–Simplified Chinese spray-name localisation.

Codebook

Overview 1 · Literal translation 2 · Semantic paraphrase 3 · Transcreation 4 · Explicitation 5 · Cultural substitution 6 · Retention / phonetic transfer

Overview

The unit of analysis is a unique English spray name and its Simplified Chinese official localisation. One primary label is assigned to each pair. The label records the dominant translation move, not every micro-change. The source form is recorded separately as single word, short phrase or clause.

Decision order: first exclude retention/phonetic transfer if the name's identity is predominantly kept in Latin script or transferred phonetically; then check whether a recognisable Chinese idiom or culture-specific expression replaces the source image; then check whether the target adds information absent from the source; then decide whether the remaining relation is literal, a paraphrase, or a transcreation. Boundary cases are resolved by asking which strategy best explains the name's effect.

Important: these are analytic categories, not claims that the translators consciously followed them. Real examples can combine strategies; the label marks the primary move.

1. Literal translation

Definition: the target keeps the source image, proposition and most lexical content, with ordinary grammatical adjustment only.

Theory source: Vinay & Darbelnet's direct procedure of literal translation; equivalence at the level of image and proposition.

Decision rule: render the English core mentally into Chinese and check whether the target preserves the same image, participants and evaluation without adding a new idea.

PositiveSad Crab → 悲伤螃蟹
PositiveRevolving Door → 旋转门

Negative example: Blep → 猫猫吐舌. The target adds a cat and an action, so it is explicitation rather than literal translation.

Boundary case: Lights Out → 熄灯 is literal in image and proposition, even though the English is idiomatic; the Chinese keeps the same light-extinguishing image.

2. Semantic paraphrase

Definition: the core meaning is retained, but wording, syntax or lexical choice is substantially rephrased.

Theory source: Vinay & Darbelnet's modulation and paraphrase; Nida's dynamic equivalence as a broad functional orientation.

Decision rule: the target says approximately the same thing as the source, but a literal back-translation would not match the original wording; no new information or strong Chinese cultural replacement is introduced.

PositiveTake a Bow → 鞠躬
PositiveWhat's That? → 什么情况？

Negative example: Break Time → 老铁困了. This creates a new informal persona and effect, so it is transcreation.

Boundary case: This is Also Fine → 这样也好 is paraphrastic rather than literal because the English evaluative phrase is recast as a Chinese conversational response.

3. Transcreation

Definition: the translator creates a new expression or image to preserve the name's playful, rhetorical or branding effect.

Theory source: Gaballo on transcreation; functionalist skopos theory; creative adaptation of brand language.

Decision rule: a literal or straightforward paraphrase would explain the source but lose the name's main effect; the target introduces a new wording, metaphor, persona or joke while keeping a recognisable function.

PositiveBreak Time → 老铁困了
PositiveNo Toxicity → 无毒无害

Negative example: Good Boy → 乖狗狗 is a close rendering of the same address and evaluation, so it is literal.

Boundary case: No Toxicity → 无毒无害 overlaps with paraphrase because the core idea of harmlessness remains. It is transcreated because the Chinese four-character pattern creates a new slogan-like effect.

4. Explicitation

Definition: the target adds information that is implicit in the source image or name, making an entity, action or context explicit.

Theory source: Blum-Kulka's explicitation hypothesis; translation shifts that clarify cohesion and context.

Decision rule: identify a concrete element in the target that is not stated in the English source and that is not required by Chinese grammar alone.

PositiveBlep → 猫猫吐舌
PositiveBawk Bawk Brimstone → 公鸡炼狱

Negative example: Sad Crab → 悲伤螃蟹 adds nothing beyond ordinary grammatical expression, so it is literal.

Boundary case: Yoonseul Frog → 高丽风情小蛙 adds cultural framing. It is classified as explicitation because the main move is to spell out the Korean association rather than replace the image with a Chinese one.

5. Cultural substitution

Definition: the target replaces a source-language image or phrase with a recognisable Chinese idiom, proverb or culture-specific expression.

Theory source: Aixelá on culture-specific items; Venuti's domestication; Chinese idiom and four-character pattern as a local rhetorical resource.

Decision rule: the target contains an established Chinese expression whose cultural form, not merely its meaning, explains the translation choice.

PositiveAll Yuck, No Yum → 味如嚼蜡
PositiveWith Sage, Comes Fame → 有贤则名

Negative example: What's That? → 什么情况？ is colloquial Chinese but not a culturally specific idiom replacing the source image.

Boundary case: With Sage, Comes Fame → 有贤则名 combines a Chinese proverb-like pattern with a character name. It is cultural substitution because the idiom is the dominant reason the target works.

6. Retention / phonetic transfer

Definition: a proper name, acronym, number or brand element is retained in Latin script or transferred phonetically.

Theory source: Aixelá's repetition and transference; proper-name translation and brand-name consistency.

Decision rule: ask whether the name's identity is carried mainly by the original Latin form, a number, or a phonetic approximation. Partial retention counts when the untranslated element remains the name's anchor.

PositiveVCT x EDG → VCT x EDG
PositiveChamber → 尚勃勒

Negative example: Jett → 捷风 is a semantic/creative Chinese rendering of the agent codename rather than phonetic transfer.

Boundary case: KAY/O Punch → K/O重拳 retains the proper name but translates the action. It is classified as retention because the identity-bearing element stays in Latin script.

总览

分析单位是一个唯一的英文喷漆名称及其官方简体中文译名。每个配对只赋予一个主要标签。标签记录主要翻译转换，而非所有微观改动。原文形式另以「单词」、「短语」或「子句」记录。

判定次序：先排除名称身份主要以拉丁字母保留或音译的「保留／音译」；再检查是否有可辨识的中文成语或文化特定表达取代原文意象；其后检查译文是否加入原文没有的信息；最后判断其余关系属于直译、语义改写或创译。边界案例以「哪一种策略最能解释名称效果」作判准。

重要：这些是分析类别，不代表译者自觉依循此分类。真实例子可同时包含多种策略；标签只标示主要转换。

1. 直译

定义：译文保留原文意象、命题及大部分词汇内容，只作一般语法调整。

理论来源：Vinay & Darbelnet 的直接翻译程序；意象与命题层面的等值。

判定规则：把英文核心直译成中文后，检查译文是否保留相同意象、参与者与评价，并且没有加入新概念。

正面例子Sad Crab → 悲伤螃蟹
正面例子Revolving Door → 旋转门

反面例子：Blep → 猫猫吐舌。译文加入猫与动作，因此属显化而非直译。

边界案例：Lights Out → 熄灯虽然英文是惯用语，但中文仍保留「熄灯」意象，因此归为直译。

2. 语义改写

定义：保留核心意思，但大幅改写措辞、句法或词汇选择。

理论来源：Vinay & Darbelnet 的调节与改写；Nida 动态等值的广泛功能取向。

判定规则：译文大致表达相同内容，但回译后与原文措辞明显不同；同时没有加入新信息，也没有明显替换为中文文化表达。

正面例子Take a Bow → 鞠躬
正面例子What's That? → 什么情况？

反面例子：Break Time → 老铁困了。译文建立新的非正式人物语气与效果，因此属创译。

边界案例：This is Also Fine → 这样也好把英文评价句改写为中文会话回应，属语义改写而非直译。

3. 创译

定义：译者创造新的表达或意象，以保留名称的趣味、修辞或品牌效果。

理论来源：Gaballo 的创译论；功能目的论；品牌语言的创造性改写。

判定规则：直译或直接改写虽能解释原文，却会失去名称的主要效果；译文引入新的措辞、比喻、人物语气或笑点，同时保留可辨识的功能。

正面例子Break Time → 老铁困了
正面例子No Toxicity → 无毒无害

反面例子：Good Boy → 乖狗狗保留相同称呼与评价，因此属直译。

边界案例：No Toxicity → 无毒无害与语义改写有重叠，因为「无害」概念保留；但其四字口号效果是新造的，因此归为创译。

4. 显化

定义：译文补充原文意象或名称中隐含的信息，使人物、动作或情境更明确。

理论来源：Blum-Kulka 的显化假设；翻译中为澄清衔接与语境而产生的转换。

判定规则：指出译文中一项英文没有明说、且不是中文语法所必需的具体元素。

正面例子Blep → 猫猫吐舌
正面例子Bawk Bawk Brimstone → 公鸡炼狱

反面例子：Sad Crab → 悲伤螃蟹除一般语法表达外没有新增内容，因此属直译。

边界案例：Yoonseul Frog → 高丽风情小蛙加入文化框架。主要转换是把韩国联想说清楚，而非以中国意象替换，因此归为显化。

5. 文化替换

定义：译文以可辨识的中文成语、谚语或文化特定表达，替换原文意象或说法。

理论来源：Aixelá 的文化特定项；Venuti 的归化；中文成语与四字格作为本地修辞资源。

判定规则：译文包含一个既有中文表达，而翻译选择需由该文化形式（不只其意义）解释。

正面例子All Yuck, No Yum → 味如嚼蜡
正面例子With Sage, Comes Fame → 有贤则名

反面例子：What's That? → 什么情况？虽是中文口语，但不是以文化特定成语替换原文意象。

边界案例：With Sage, Comes Fame → 有贤则名结合中文谚语式结构与角色名。成语是译文成立的主要原因，因此归为文化替换。

6. 保留／音译

定义：专名、缩写、数字或品牌元素以拉丁字母保留，或以音译方式转入中文。

理论来源：Aixelá 的重复与转移；专名翻译与品牌名称一致性。

判定规则：判断名称身份是否主要由原有拉丁形式、数字或语音近似承载。若未翻译元素仍是名称核心，则属部分保留。

正面例子VCT x EDG → VCT x EDG
正面例子Chamber → 尚勃勒

反面例子：Jett → 捷风是对特工代号的语义／创意中文处理，不是音译。

边界案例：KAY/O Punch → K/O重拳保留专名但翻译动作。由于身份核心仍在拉丁字母，因此归为保留。
